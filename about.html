About me

Since my childhood, I have worked hard to overcome my fears and learn difficult things. It has been a tough but exciting journey. My name is Shubham Jain, and I was born in New Delhi, India. Now, I live in Calgary, Canada, where I am building a career that combines accounting, finance, and technology.

When I went to school in India, my classmates often laughed at how I spoke in English. I used to stammer and make grammar mistakes, and they made fun of me for it. This made me too scared to try to get better, and I developed a deep fear of speaking English. In 2016, I moved to Charlottetown, Canada, and faced a new problem. Because of my school fears, I still could not speak English with confidence. It was so hard that I struggled to make friends or even order food at a restaurant. But I refused to let fear control me. I learned that the best way to beat a huge challenge is to break it into small, easy steps.

I used this same idea of breaking things down in my career. I got my Bachelor's degree in Accounting and started my CPA training. Over four years of working in Calgary, I managed large amounts of financial data. I used NetSuite to carefully review numbers and ensure everything was correct at the end of each month. I learned to love working with data, finding mistakes, and organizing messy processes. I realized something important: behind every number is a bigger business story. But while accounting taught me how to record the past, I wanted to learn how to predict the future.

In July 2024, a new chapter began. While exploring AI tools on YouTube, I came across a video about building your own AI models. What intrigued me was that I could start learning Python first, which is a comparatively easier language to begin the journey into the AI field. Watching practical tutorials and step-by-step guides convinced me that learning Python would be the fastest way to build useful tools. I promised myself to dedicate at least 30 minutes per day for a month to see if I was truly committed to this new path. I started with the book called Python Crash Course by Eric Matthes. I used ideas from the book Atomic Habits: making a little progress every day and practicing regularly. Eventually, those 30 minutes turned into one hour, then two, then three, and, consequently, my curiosity led me deeper down the path toward data science. I realized that to truly learn machine learning, I needed to understand linear algebra, matrices, vectors, and statistics. So, I went back to basics and started taking math lessons from Khan Academy, which was the best free option available.

Once I finished the prerequisites of machine learning, I completed several Kaggle micro-courses covering Intro to ML, Intermediate ML, Time Series, Feature Engineering, and Intro to Deep Learning. I then began taking part in Kaggle Playground competitions. I love these competitions because I get to see how smart people from all over the world solve problems. The best way to learn was to read what others wrote in the forums, see their ideas about the data, and try their methods myself. You learn so much from people around the world when you are open to new ideas.

Today, I am a mix of two worlds. I connect business with technology. I can talk about financial reports and numbers just as easily as I can write Python code and SQL queries. I focus on machine learning, AI, and showing data clearly. I believe data is more than just numbers on a screen. Every set of data tells a story. My goal is to keep learning, keep getting better, and use my unique skills to build tools that help society.
